Part 2: a worked mechanic for Fika & Co

One possible answer. Students' answers will differ; check them against the rules, not against this text.

Step 5: smallest acceptable truth

"The coffee arrives before you run out."

Seven words, no feature list, says something about the person: they are someone who never runs out.

Step 6: the mechanic

The CChallenge, with a touch of Conformity.
What the customer doesAfter the second delivery, picks one colleague "who always steals your coffee" and sends them a bag.
What the friend receivesA personal message with the customer's name and a link to claim a 250 g bag for 1 kr, delivered with the customer's next box.
Limit on peopleOne colleague per delivery.
Limit on time48 hours to claim, or the bag goes into the customer's own box.
For whomThe 9's: it gives them status at the coffee machine.

Step 7: the two messages

To the customer: "Your next box has one extra bag in it. Send it to the colleague who always steals your coffee. If they don't claim it in 48 hours, it stays with you."

To the friend: "Anna says you keep stealing her coffee. She is sending you your own bag: the coffee arrives before you run out. Claim it within 48 hours or it stays with Anna."

Step 8: the friend through the formula

MThey drink coffee at work every day and run out at home.
VA real bag of good coffee for 1 kr, and a joke to answer.
I48 hours, then Anna keeps it.
FCreating an account before seeing what they get. Fix: claim with an email address only; the account comes later.
A"Is this a subscription trap?" Fix: the trust artifact is Anna's name plus the line "no subscription, cancel nothing, it is one bag".

Step 9: the share card

ME: "I never run out of coffee. Anna made sure. Your turn." A card that says something about the sender. A VS version, "light or dark? pick one and it is on its way", is the cheapest to build.

Step 10: back in the sheet

The mechanic sets invites per inviter to 1 per delivery (monthly), but raises the share who invite and the invite conversion, because the invitation is personal and free. A reasonable new set: 0.4 × 0.6 × 1 × 0.5 = K 0.12 per month, compounding every month for as long as the customer stays. Cycle time is one delivery.

Sample size to detect 5.0 % → 6.5 % at 5 % significance and 80 % power: about 3 800 visitors per variant, 7 600 in total. The A/B calculator says 3 780.

Step 19: the decision, filled in

Hypothesis: If we change the hero button to "Get your first bag for 1 kr" for all home page visitors, the signup rate goes up because the incentive is concrete and the price anxiety drops.
Result: A 5.0 % vs B 6.5 %, n = 4 000 per variant, p = 0.004.
Decision: ship B, because p is below 0.05 and a 30 % relative uplift is worth a one-line copy change.
Next test: the signup form itself, where 6.5 % becomes the new baseline.

In the Growth loop sheet, invite conversion goes from 0.25 to 0.325 (×1.3). K goes from 0.15 to 0.195. Same uplift on the rate, same uplift on the loop.

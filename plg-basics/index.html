Growth ToolboxLoops, mechanics, testsJesper Åström

Two formulas frame the day. The conversion formula from The System decides whether one person acts. K decides whether the people who act bring more people. Everything else is how you find out which is true: a sheet, a worksheet, an A/B test. Two hours: 20 minutes intro, then 25, 35, 30 and 10 minutes for the four parts.

Intro

What is product-led growth?

The product is the main channel. Users bring users.

Marketing-led or sales-led

  • Ads and salespeople bring every new user. Each one costs money again.
  • The product sits behind a demo, a quote or a paywall.
  • Growth stops the day the budget stops.

Product-led

  • People can try the product on their own: free plan, free trial, self-serve signup.
  • Using the product exposes it to other people, or gives users a reason to invite them.
  • Marketing's job becomes: remove friction and design the loops.
In one sentence The fastest way to the next user is a current user doing something in the product.
Examples the students know: Spotify, Dropbox, Slack, Figma, Zoom, Duolingo, Canva, Calendly, Notion. Ask: how did you get your first Spotify account? Nearly everyone answers with a person, not an ad. That is the whole idea.

Intro

Funnel or loop?

A funnel ends. A loop feeds itself.

New userarrives
Activatesgets the value
Does somethinginvites, shares, creates
Others see itand some arrive
the output is the next input
1 000 visitors
50 sign up
20 stay

A funnel: to get the next 20 you buy the next 1 000.

The question to ask about any product What does a happy user do that a stranger can see?
The funnel is still true inside each cycle of the loop: people drop off at every step. The difference is what happens at the bottom. In a loop, the people who come out the bottom are the input of the next cycle. Part 3 tests one step of the funnel; Part 1 models the loop around it.

Same shape, different thing the user does.

The first three are product-led. The fourth is what most companies actually run, and it is still a loop: it only compounds if payback is faster than the cash runs out. Today we model the invite loop because it is the one you can design on a whiteboard in an afternoon.

Intro

The network you grow

From The System: the internet is content, people and the links between them.

  • Every loop adds to one of three assets: content, people or links. Own more of them and the next launch is easier.
  • Your network is not one crowd. It is 1's, 9's and 90's.
  • Communicate through people, not to them. A loop is that sentence, built into the product.

Intro

Two numbers decide a loop

How many new users one user brings, and how long that takes.

K = i × ci = invitations one user sends · c = share of invitations that become a user

Break i down further when you model it: activation rate × share of activated users who invite × invites per inviter.

KWhat one paid user is worth
0.151.2 users
0.52 users
0.85 users
1.0 or moreGrows on its own. Very rare, and never for long.

Below 1, the loop multiplies every user you bring in: total = input ÷ (1 − K). Cycle time decides how fast: a loop that turns in days beats one that turns in months.

Kill the myth first: almost no product has K above 1. A loop with K of 0.5 is a great business because it doubles everything else you do. The exercise in Part 1 makes this concrete: they will watch total users flatten at 1000 ÷ (1 − K).

Part 2Theory

The conversion formula

Whether one person does what you want. Motivation first.

C = M + V + (I − F) − AConversion = Motivation + Value proposition + (Incentive − Friction) − Anxiety
MA need they already have. You cannot create it this quarter.
VWhat they perceive they get when they act.
IWhy now and not later. Time limits, limited editions.
FEverything that makes the action hard. Find it by watching, not asking.
AThe fear of a wrong choice. Find it by asking. Fix with trust artifacts: reviews, logos, names.
Only three things to change the design, the copy, or the call to action. Then prototype, test, observe, learn, prototype again.
The formula is Stefan Helgesson's, used throughout The System. Key teaching: motivation is the base, and it is the one you cannot buy. The book's method for friction is recordings and cursor tracking; for anxiety it is interviews where you read the answer back until the real reason comes out. In Part 2 they fill the five letters in for the friend who receives an invitation.

Part 2Theory

The smallest acceptable truth

The one sentence people use to explain your product to each other.

  • Facebook had profiles, groups, causes, a chat. People said: "you can see if anyone is single." Later: "you were tagged in a photo."
  • It does not have to be the full story, or even fully true. It has to be usable in a conversation and acceptable to the person saying it.
  • Find it by watching what your 1's say, test the candidates on a few 9's, keep the one that moves, amplify it everywhere.

For Fika & Co

Not: "a coffee subscription with three roasts, roasted the week we ship, flexible rhythm."

Maybe: "the coffee arrives before you run out."

What a customer says at the coffee machine. The sentence the invite has to carry.

This is the bridge between the loop math and the copy. Invite conversion in the sheet is mostly whether the message the friend receives contains the smallest acceptable truth. Corporate front pages fail here because everyone won the war for the headline.

Part 2Theory

Through-content

What the share says about the sharer. Three kinds of message that travel through people.

WOW

A detail that makes the sharer look like they know something first.

Feel, think, grow. Fits the creativity principle.

ME

Says who the sharer is. A result, a type, a badge.

"I am…". Fits conformity. Quiz results, Wrapped, streaks.

VS

Two options, pick one. Answering costs nothing.

From the through-content section of The System. In the worksheet, step 9 asks which of the three the share card is. Most product referral programs are none of them: they are "give 10 EUR, get 10 EUR", which is pure incentive with nothing about the sharer. That is why they convert badly among 9's.

Part 3Theory

What an A/B test is

Two versions, a random split, one metric, enough data to trust the difference.

  • Hypothesis first: "If we change X for these visitors, this metric will go up, because reason."
  • One variable. Today: "Start your subscription" becomes "Get your first bag for 1 kr". Nothing else.
  • Random split, and the same visitor always sees the same version.
  • Primary metric decided before you start. Sample size decided before you start.

Today's test on Fika & Co

ChangeThe hero button text
For whomAll home page visitors, 50/50
Funnelassigned → hero click → signup
Metricsignups ÷ assigned
BecauseIncentive up, price anxiety down
Tie it to Part 2: this is a change in V and I for the visitor, measured. The funnel has three events, all of which already exist in their container from the Tag Manager lecture except the first. Today we add the first and attach the variant to the other two.

Part 3Theory

Six rules that save a test

Every one of these has ruined a real test somewhere.

  1. Decide the metric and the sample size before you start.
  2. Do not peek every day and stop when it looks good. Run to the sample size.
  3. Never change the variants while the test runs.
  1. Run full weeks. Tuesday visitors are not Saturday visitors.
  2. Segment after, not before. Mobile and desktop can differ; that is a finding, not a filter.
  3. Write down the decision rule in advance: ship B if p is below 0.05 and the uplift is above what it costs to change.
The one number you will learn to read the p-value: how likely a difference this big is if the two versions are really the same. Below 0.05 is the usual bar.
Rule 2 is the one students break in their first job: with daily peeking, a test of two identical versions "wins" about a third of the time. Part 4 shows the same rates being not significant at 1000 visitors and significant at 4000.

<script>
(function () {
  var forced = (location.search.match(/[?&]ab=([AB])/) || [])[1];
